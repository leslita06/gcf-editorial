Roméo Mivekannin, “Dieu n'est pas obligé”

5 November 2026 – 9 January 2027Abidjan, Cocody
For immediate release
Abidjan, 17 November 2026

For his third solo exhibition in Côte d'Ivoire, Roméo Mivekannin presents at Galerie Cécile Fakhoury a pantheon of rituals and images painted on black velvet, spanning West Africa, Europe and Brazil.

Through a pantheon of rituals and images, Roméo Mivekannin gives shape to a quest marked by manifestations of the sacred, the metamorphoses of gestures and the ambivalence of religion. His figures emerge from the depth of black velvet: rites of passage, visions of sacrifice and initiation, drawn from Baroque painting, colonial archives and contemporary photography.

This new Ivorian opus, painted mostly in white acrylic, follows the exhibitions Les âmes du peuple noir (2020) and Effractions (2022) in Abidjan. It comes after residencies and exhibitions that shaped the artist's path, notably at the Collezione Maramotti, where his velvet painting technique was born, and in Salvador de Bahia and São Paulo, exploring Afro-Brazilian history and cultures.

A title borrowed from Ahmadou Kourouma

By placing the exhibition under the aegis of Ahmadou Kourouma's novel Allah n'est pas obligé, the artist sets his new paintings in an ambivalence he describes as “of blood and honey”. Au nom du Christ, after Roger Gnoan Mbala (1993) takes up a night baptism scene from the Ivorian director's parable film.

The black velvet, hanging free of any stretcher, was chosen by the artist because it offers “the deepest possible black, without any reflection”. It becomes a mysterious border from which initiates, dancers and priestesses emerge.

Also on view

Born in Bouaké in 1986, Roméo Mivekannin lives and works between Toulouse and Cotonou. His work is also shown in Brussels, in “An infinite woman, black archives in two acts” at Kanal – Centre Pompidou, from 28 November 2026.

« Each of Roméo Mivekannin's works is a moment of balance: a figure standing at the edge of the night it comes from. »Galerie Cécile Fakhoury Example
